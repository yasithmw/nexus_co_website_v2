The first service page (Marketing & Brand) was written May 2026. The second (Software Development) was written May 2026. The third (AI & Machine Learning) was written May 2026. The fourth (Resource Augmentation) was written May 2026. All four establish these patterns:

- Hero headline: declarative statement, 5–7 words, outcome-focused. Not a question, not a tagline — a confident claim about what the work produces.
- Overview headline: short fragment in "Built to be X." style, echoing existing site patterns.
- FAQ answers: 2–4 sentences, direct — the first sentence is the answer, the rest add nuance. No hedging openers.
- Testimonials: always include one skeptic-turned-client quote (the conversion arc is more persuasive than pure endorsement). Outcomes are specific (timeline in weeks, percentage improvement, a decision saved).
- Approach steps: three phases, discovery → strategy+creative → launch+retain. This structure should be consistent across all service pages.

**Why:** Consistency across service pages reduces decision-making overhead on future pages. The skeptic testimonial format was a deliberate choice — it does more persuasive work than three satisfied-client quotes.

**How to apply:** All four service pages are now complete. Resource Augmentation page (May 2026) reinforced the "senior" credentialling device with a concrete definition (8+ years production experience, not years of doing junior work) — that specificity pattern is worth carrying to any future page where quality claims need defending. The three testimonial arc used: fast-need-met, traditional-hiring-failed-then-us (skeptic-turned-client), and long-term-embedded (retention proof). That arc is the most persuasive sequence for B2B service pages with a trust gap. Software Development page used "senior engineers" credentialling as a recurring device — useful pattern for any service page where team quality is a key differentiator. AI/ML page introduced a "build and validate in real conditions" framing for phase 2 — emphasising production data over clean samples. That contrast (real vs. lab) is worth reusing when a service discipline has a strong anti-hype angle. See [[project-nexus-brand]] for voice rules.
